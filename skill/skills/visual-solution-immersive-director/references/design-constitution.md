# Design Constitution

## Core Standard

Create commercially useful websites with high visual cohesion, compelling motion, clear information, and strong runtime feel. The target is not a fixed aesthetic; the target is consistently high production value appropriate to the client.

## User Taste Model

Prioritize problems in this order:
1. Bad motion/scroll feel: stutter, drag, awkward pacing, forced waits, sticky friction, disjointed transitions.
2. Safe or boring creative direction with insufficient surprise or memorability.
3. Weak brand specificity or a site that could belong to any company.

Never sacrifice performance or interaction feel merely to preserve an effect.

## Visual World

- Make the site feel like one world, not a sequence of unrelated components.
- Adapt density to the brand. Rich, layered compositions are welcome when appropriate; minimalism is valid when the brand calls for it.
- Avoid generic AI/SaaS composition as a default.
- Use brand/product/character/scene/photo assets as primary visual material.
- Add lighting, texture, depth, 3D, particles, masks, shaders, or ambient effects only when they strengthen the world.

## Motion Language

Favor cinematic, continuous, scroll-driven behavior when suited to the project:
- zoom/push/pull,
- camera-like movement,
- spatial transitions,
- foreground/midground/background speed differences,
- scale and depth shifts,
- masking/reveals,
- controlled pinned sequences,
- continuity between scenes.

Motion should feel choreographed, not appended after layout.

Use a mixed pace:
- slow down for key emotional/brand moments,
- stay responsive during normal browsing,
- never force users to wait for decorative animation.

## Story Rhythm

Do not make every section a climax.

Create:
- 2-3 memorable hero/wow moments when appropriate,
- quieter reading passages,
- visual breathing room,
- alternating intensity,
- deliberate transitions between informational and experiential sections.

Judge each scene by its role in the whole site's rhythm, not only by local spectacle.

## Hero

Treat the first screen as a critical quality gate.
It must quickly establish a memorable first impression, the brand world, and a reason to continue.
The hero may be immediately powerful or restrained before unfolding through scroll; choose based on the brand.

## Information

Blend immersive storytelling with stable information presentation.
- Keep core commercial content, product facts, service explanations, CTAs, and decision-critical information easy to understand.
- Use more experimental presentation for brand emotion, story, and transitions.
- Do not hide required information behind interaction gimmicks.

## Micro-interaction

Add hover, cursor, card, navigation, and button behaviors only when they reinforce hierarchy, tactility, narrative, or brand character.

## Audio

Do not depend on music. The experience must work fully in silence.

## Loading

Use a loader/opening only when it:
- covers real loading,
- introduces the world,
- prepares a transition into the hero,
- or creates meaningful anticipation.

Avoid artificial waiting.

## Desktop Stage

The default production and approval target is 2560×1280. Resolve composition, interaction feel and performance at that viewport before expanding scope. Do not design or judge Tablet／Mobile in this stage. If the user later requests RWD, define its own priorities and acceptance criteria instead of treating desktop approval as device approval.

## Page Investment

Allocate the highest creative investment to:
1. homepage,
2. one hero inner page.

Other inner pages should prioritize reading and clear information while maintaining the same brand system.
